# On Dance

You need to listen to the beat.  I reflexively smiled after hearing that, and kept trying my best to hear this "beat" and move my body to it.  Hearing that<that|accusation|phrase> didn't help what was going on internally in my brain, but I kept trying.  I'd love to just turn off my thoughts and just be with the vibe and have no worries; just be in the music, movement, and community.  However, I was a bit overwhelmed; I didn't know less than a minute ago I'd be dancing to the songs of a DJ on an empty dance floor with someone I don't know.  Not didn't know, don't know.  I still don't know this person, and that's okay; they definitely left an impact on me.  You see, after the first comment, we continued dancing a few feet away for a few more seconds before she repeated it.  You need to listen to the beat.  We both just stopped, looked at each other, then I put my head down and walked to the table with all my friends. "Charlie Browning" it back to the table.  After I took my seat, she came over to my friends and said "your friend needs to listen to the beat".  We all laughed and they gave me some more shit.  A minute earlier they all had outed me and sent me on this side quest.  The dancer had came to our table and asked us all "which one of ya'll are single?" Them all being couples unanimously pointed to me, the only single person.  She had me join her on the dance floor for that minute, not knowing that I would disappoint her and not be what she was looking for.  You can think this was a negative event, and it was, but it wasn't terrible; it's something I laughed about then and still do to this day.  It should be noted that dance is something memorable, whether negative or positive; you want it to be positive, but it can take effort and community to get there.  That event didn't discourage me, as I still went out and wiggled in clubs a few times since then.  It always stuck with me though; I like to improve, as I don't feel like I'm the person I'm meant to be yet.  It's not a pressure I put on myself though, instead I open to being conscious of my self (faults and not) and open to saying yes to things that feel like the next step in my life.  It wouldn't be until a decade after that event that I could say I'm getting close to dancing to the beat.

## The Journey

It's getting close to a year since I started partner dancing; as I write this, I'm going into my first summer of dance. If you're wondering if someone who has been doing something for year almost daily and writing about said something actually enjoys it or not, I love it. I absolutely fucking love dancing. I had no clue this is the hobby I would pick up and that I'd feel such joy and community. <find better segway here> My therapist has been pushing me to find a hobby where there is an in-person community to better align with my goals.  For the majority of my adult life, I have been behind a screen playing video games online with friends.  I'm responsible about it; I focused in school to get good grades, took advantage of internships in college, which lead to opportunities for my career. Doing all the stuff you gotta do to survive in this world. But on my free time, I often chose on-line video games in the comfort of my room. Logically, I knew I'm going to get the most out of life by being out in the community, but video games offered me a lot and it was a low bar to entry. Finding groups you really enjoy can take time and effort, and I have a group of friends from high school that I really enjoy goofing off in video games with.

I chose to start cutting back and spending more time in the real world -- waking up out of The Matrix yo. I'm the type that is content with just existing, and I'm thankful for anything extra.  I wasn't really enjoying some of the meet-ups I'd found, so I decided to do what people have been doing for centuries... I went to a local tavern.  I have a good selection of bars near me, but I found one that was worth the 5 minute walk to the 10 minute bus ride.  I'd hit that up almost every night when I was working from home; I'd sit at the bar, watch what they'd have on the TV, periodically play on my phone, try to be a delightful guest to the staff, order drinks and the occasional food.  I'd just sit there; some nights I'd have wonderful conversations, other nights I might go without speaking to anyone.  But I just kept showing up, getting familiar being outside my room. Me being out almost every night was a fun subject with my friends; I'd snap them a photo most nights so they'd get a little taste of the experience.

The other thing I'd do with my free time in the real world was playing table-top role playing games with a great group of friends.  Some of these friends also liked to get out and knew I was going out a lot now.  They'd find fun shows to go to around town; sometimes it would be a bigger show (like a $20-30 show), most times we'd see a $5-10 show. For us we didn't want to go to the coliseum for huge shows that could be $100+, sit in chairs, over pay for drinks, and have it be a whole event.  We'll do a big coliseum show here and there, but on the regular, we just wanted to meet up and chill with each other and have a more affordable and intimate moment of music. It's magical and was very surreal for me at first being able to walk into a small venue and have someone just get up in front of us and put on an enjoyable show that showcases their hard work and effort. Being human, you can recognize how these people had to spend their time to make the event happen. I know that I'm buying a ticket or just tipping the band and they're doing a performance for us, but I just feel so thankful that they are making a nice experience in the world.

When you go out to music, you're eventually going to stumble into a song that's going to make you want to wiggle.  If you haven't, but you're having a good time, like you're not off-put by the idea and experience of music, I'd say just keep trying and keep showing up; you're going to find something that makes your feet move.  That's what I just started doing.  I was vibing with the music, and I'd just kinda wiggle my body and slightly move my feet. Different music would make me get more into the wiggle and feel more free to move. It started enhancing the music. I might be the first to say this but I am going to state that dancing is a music enhancer (also that wiggling is dancing -- come at me). It enhanced the music so much that I didn't care what others were thinking of me.  I just wanted to exist and be in the same room as live music and feel it; if my body wanted me to move to it then I'm going to listen to it. I started to feel like, my body must naturally want to wiggle to the beat, and with enough practice at this.  I thought maybe I was.  I have no empirical data, so I don't really want to state that I did dance to the beat (something happened that I'll get to later that makes me question my beat powers).

So while I was appeasing my therapist and having a good time doing it, I did not know how much better it could get.  You never know what chains of events are going to be life changing, and how innocuous it can be.  A friend who knows my journey, who knows I'm down for going out and I'm always out just sitting on a stool at a bar, reached out to me.  She told me one of her friends got caught up in work and wanted to go to this country show.  I don't mind being second pick, I like being thought of, and I saw it as opportunity.  When some plans don't work out, it can become an opportunity for others; be open to saying yes.  Well I arrived at this show, early to mingle, and my friend introduced me to James <use this name?>.  James is down with music; they're in a band and runs an account that helps people find the more honky tonk music around.  Music eventually starts, I throw my ear protection in, and wiggle away.  Then some real fuckery starts up; people just start pairing up and doing a conjoined upgraded wiggle around the floor.  It was was wonderful to watch; they're joy with the dancing and music was contagious and helped me feel even more joy wiggling and listening to this band.  Learned that night that James and another person Robbie (who no one introduced me to and I was too shy to say hello) were starting up some more structured dance lessons that were once a week for four weeks. My friend was going to go to some of them, invited me, and I had just met James so I knew some familiar faces. What do you think my answer was? I definitely said no didn't I? Like why would I want to do something that looks fun with some nice people, it might be embarrassing, and I was gonna be bad at it.  That's the point, get comfortable with that feeling of embarrassment until you realize it's not embarrassing at all; and you're there to learn, you're going to be bad.  But you're not a bad dancer if both you and your partner are smiling and having a genuinely good time.  I'm all about saying yes when an opportunity presents itself and it feels like the next natural thing for me. So I said yes. Say yes to the dance.
